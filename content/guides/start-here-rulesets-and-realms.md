## The short version

- **Ruleset first, then faction.** You can only group with players on the same ruleset and the same faction.
- **Pick the faction your friends pick.** Cross-faction grouping is not a thing.
- **Meet at level 10.** Starting zones are race-specific; the first shared hub is level 10.
- **Agree on a pace before launch.** Different play speeds break groups faster than bad specs.

## Why the ruleset comes before anything else

Forever replaces the classic server list with **rulesets**. Your ruleset decides how the open world treats you, who you can group with, and what happens when you die. Changing your mind later means rerolling.

| Ruleset | What it means in practice | Pick it if… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PvE (Normal)** | Open-world PvP is opt-in. You can quest in peace and queue for battlegrounds when you want them. | You want steady leveling and dungeon focus. |
| **PvP** | Your PvP flag stays on in contested zones. Enemy players can attack you at any time. | You enjoy contested zones and world fights. |
| **RP** | Roleplay naming and community expectations. | You want an RP community and slower, social play. |
| **RP-PvP** | Roleplay rules plus permanent world PvP. Added for launch. | Your RP guild wants world PvP friction. |
| **Hardcore** | Death is permanent. Community reporting says this ruleset arrives after launch, not day one. | You want the one-life challenge — later. |

The important part: **same ruleset + same faction = groupable.** Everything else is downstream of that.

## Faction is the real lock

If your friends roll Horde, you roll Horde. There is no cross-faction group finder, no shared auction house, no "we'll meet in the middle." This sounds obvious until launch week, when someone picks a night elf because the starting zone looks nicer.

Decide as a group:

1. **Faction** — locked to your friends.
2. **Ruleset** — locked to your friends.
3. **Starting zone** — flexible, because you can meet at 10.

## Where your group meets at level 10

Each race starts in its own zone. The first practical meetup is level 10, in the faction hub:

- **Alliance:** humans start in Elwynn Forest, dwarves and gnomes in Dun Morogh, night elves in Teldrassil. At 10, converge on **Stormwind** or **Westfall**.
- **Horde:** orcs and trolls start in Durotar, tauren in Mulgore, undead in Tirisfal Glades. At 10, converge on **Orgrimmar** or **the Barrens**.

Waiting until 10 is usually better than forcing everyone into one starting zone: the 1–10 experience is short, and nobody wants to reroll a race they dislike just to share a spawn point.

## Five-player composition

A standard dungeon group is **one tank, one healer, three damage**. Beyond that:

- Avoid stacking the same class. Loot competition is the fastest way to sour a launch group.
- Hybrids (druid, paladin, shaman, priest) can flex, but pick a primary role so gearing stays sane.
- Decide who levels **First Aid** and who levels **Cooking** — the group needs both, and nobody should assume someone else is doing it.

## The launch-day checklist

- **Install and patch the client before launch day.** Do not spend launch hour downloading.
- **Set up addons and keybinds in advance.** Quest and map addons are the big ones; a group where half the players can see quest objectives and half cannot will fall apart.
- **Agree on an offline policy.** Does the group wait for the person with a job, or does everyone play ahead? Decide before, not at level 14.
- **Expect launch-hour instability.** Queues and layer issues are normal on day one. Have a solo activity ready (professions, bag space, banking) for the rough patch.
