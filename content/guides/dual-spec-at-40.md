## What dual spec is

At level 40, your character unlocks a **second talent configuration**. The point is simple: one spec for solo leveling and farming, one spec for dungeons and group content, without paying a full respec every time you change plans.

That changes how you should think about the 1–40 stretch. Before 40, commit to one path and make it work. After 40, your character has two jobs.

## Why it matters more on Forever

Classic characters usually level as one thing and raid as another, paying for it every week. Dual spec removes that tax at exactly the level where dungeons, elite quests, and the first real group content start demanding a different toolkit.

The practical effect:

- A warrior can level as Fury and tank dungeons without a respec fee.
- A priest can solo as Shadow and heal a dungeon the same evening.
- A druid can keep a feral set for the open world and a restoration set for groups.

## The safe pattern: one solo spec, one group spec

| Class | Solo spec | Group spec | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warrior | Fury | Protection | Protection tanking is the group role; do not try to tank in Fury gear. |
| Paladin | Retribution | Holy | Healing is the reliable group role. Protection viability depends on the build — verify in game. |
| Hunter | Beast Mastery | Marksmanship | Pet spec levels; marksman spec raids. |
| Rogue | Combat | Combat (PvP: Subtlety) | Rogues rarely need a second PvE spec; use the slot for PvP. |
| Priest | Shadow | Holy or Discipline | Healers are always wanted; Shadow wants hit gear before it feels good. |
| Shaman | Enhancement | Restoration | Enhancement levels; restoration gets groups. |
| Mage | Frost | Fire | Frost for safe solo and AoE; fire for single-target raid damage. |
| Warlock | Affliction | Demonology or Destruction | Affliction for sustain; the second spec is a group-damage pick. |
| Druid | Feral (cat) | Restoration or Feral (bear) | The most flexible slot in the game — decide your primary role first. |

## Do not split your gear

The classic dual-spec mistake is trying to gear both specs at once and ending up mediocre at both. Pick one **primary role** and let the second spec borrow gear where it can.

- A warrior's tank set and DPS set overlap on plate but not on weapons or trinkets.
- A priest's Shadow set and healing set barely overlap at all — plan bank space.
- A druid's feral and caster sets are completely different; commit to one.

## When to switch

- **At 40:** unlock the second spec and set it up immediately, even if you keep leveling in your solo spec. The cost of doing it later is forgetting what you wanted.
- **Before your first raid attunement:** your group spec should be ready before you commit to a raid schedule.
- **Not mid-dungeon.** Plan switches around content, not during it.

## Common mistakes

1. **Leveling a "do everything" hybrid** and being decent at nothing. Pick a main role.
2. **Carrying two full gear sets at 40** with no bank space. Bank the off-set, not the main set.
3. **Assuming the second spec is free to maintain.** Consumables and weapon oils still cost gold.
4. **Switching specs to chase a group** that needs a role you have never played. A bad tank is worse than no tank.

## Verify at launch

Dual spec details are the kind of system Blizzard tunes right up to launch. When you hit 40, check the trainer for:

- the gold cost of unlocking the second spec,
- whether switching has a cooldown,
- whether both specs share talent-point totals or are independent.

If any of that differs from the beta reports, the pattern above still holds — only the price changes.
