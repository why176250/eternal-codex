## The week-one economy

On launch week, nobody has gold and everybody needs the same things: bag space, potions, ammo, weapon stones, and food buffs. The people who make money are not farming rare drops — they are selling the boring consumables everyone buys repeatedly.

## What sells first

1. **Bags.** Tailoring bags are the highest-demand item of the first week. Everyone needs inventory space, and cloth is everywhere.
2. **Potions and elixirs.** Dungeon groups buy healing and mana potions continuously. Alchemy pays for itself fast.
3. **Enchanting materials.** Greens nobody needs become dust and essences. An enchanter turns trash into gold.
4. **Ammo and weapon stones.** Hunters and melee always need them; engineering and blacksmithing supply them.
5. **Food with buffs.** Group food sells once players start running dungeons seriously.
6. **Cloth, herbs, and ore.** Raw materials move fast; the margin is thin but the volume is huge.

## Profession plays

- **Tailoring:** bags first, then cloth gear. The bag market is the most reliable week-one income.
- **Alchemy:** potions are consumables, and consumables sell every day. Buy herbs cheap in the morning, sell potions at night.
- **Enchanting:** disenchant dungeon greens; sell the materials, not the service, until you have a reputation.
- **Mining + Blacksmithing:** weapon stones and low-level gear; slower margin than bags, but steady.
- **Skinning + Leatherworking:** leather armor and bags; depends on how many leather wearers are leveling.

## Farming that works on launch week

- **Humanoid cloth farms.** Scarlet Monastery courtyards and similar dense humanoid camps drop cloth and greens — both sell.
- **Dungeon trash.** BRD and LBRS trash drops sell well once players reach 52–60.
- **Herb and ore routes.** Contested on day one; go at off-peak or pick a quieter zone.
- **Fishing.** Idle income, and cooking materials sell to raiders once buff food matters.

## Auction house tactics

- **Sell in stacks that match use.** One stack of 20 herbs sells faster than 20 single auctions.
- **Do not start price wars.** Undercutting by 1 copper is fine; crashing a market hurts you first.
- **Watch deposit fees.** A failed auction still costs money; do not list 40 stacks of a slow item.
- **Sell on raid nights.** Consumables peak when groups are forming.

## What not to do

- **Do not buy gold.** It is against the rules and gets accounts banned.
- **Do not join GDKP runs** if the ruleset bans them — and Forever's rulesets do restrict RMT-adjacent behavior.
- **Do not dump your first 100 gold into a profession.** Level the profession with materials you gather; buy skill-ups only where they are cheap.
- **Do not ignore repair costs.** A raider who cannot repair is a raider who wipes the group.
